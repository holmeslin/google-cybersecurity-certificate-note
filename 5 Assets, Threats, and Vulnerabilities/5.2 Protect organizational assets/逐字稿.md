### Security controls 安全控制措施

These days, information is in so many places at once.

現今，資訊同時存在於許多地方。

As a result, organizations are under a lot of pressure to implement effective security controls that protects everyone's information from being stolen or exposed.

因此，組織面臨著實施有效安全控制措施的巨大壓力，以保護每個人的資訊免遭竊取或洩露。

Security controls are safeguards designed to reduce specific security risks.

安全控制措施是旨在降低特定安全風險的保護措施。

They include a wide range of tools that protect assets before, during, and after an event.

它們包括在事件發生之前、期間和之後保護資產的各種工具。

Security controls can be organized into three types: technical, operational, and managerial.

安全控制措施可以分為三種類型：技術性、營運性和管理性。

Technical control types include the many technologies used to protect assets.

技術性控制類型包括用於保護資產的許多技術。

This includes encryption, authentication systems, and others.

這包括加密、身份驗證系統等。

Operational controls relate to maintaining the day-to-day security environment.

營運性控制與維護日常安全環境有關。

Generally, people perform these controls like awareness training and incident response.

通常，人們會執行這些控制，例如意識培訓和事件回應。

Managerial controls are centered around how the other two reduce risk.

管理性控制的重點是前兩者如何降低風險。

Examples of management controls include policies, standards, and procedures.

管理控制的範例包括政策、標準和程序。

Typically, organization's security policy outlines the controls needed to achieve their goals.

通常，組織的安全政策會概述實現其目標所需的控制措施。

Information privacy plays a key role in these decisions.

資訊隱私在這些決策中起著關鍵作用。

Information privacy is the protection of unauthorized access and distribution of data.

資訊隱私是保護未經授權的資料存取和散布。

Information privacy is about the right to choose.

資訊隱私關乎選擇的權利。

People and organizations alike deserve the right to decide when, how, and to what extent private information about them is shared.

個人和組織都應該有權決定何時、如何以及在多大程度上分享有關他們的私人資訊。

Security controls are the technologies used to regulate information privacy.

安全控制措施是用於規範資訊隱私的技術。

For example, imagine using a travel app to book a flight.

例如，想像一下使用旅遊應用程式預訂航班。

You might browse through a list of flights and find one at a good price.

你可能會瀏覽航班列表並找到價格合適的航班。

To reserve a seat, you enter some personal information, like your name, email, and credit card number for payment.

要預訂座位，你需要輸入一些個人資訊，例如你的姓名、電子郵件和信用卡號碼以進行付款。

The transaction goes through successfully, and you booked your flight.

交易成功完成，你已預訂了航班。

Now, you reasonably expect the airline company to access this information you enter when signing up to complete the reservation.

現在，你有理由期望航空公司在你註冊以完成預訂時存取你輸入的這些資訊。

However, should everyone at the company have access to your information? A person working in the marketing department shouldn't need access to your credit card information.

但是，公司裡的每個人都應該可以存取你的資訊嗎？在行銷部門工作的人不需要存取你的信用卡資訊。

It makes sense to share that information with a customer support agent.

與客戶支援專員分享該資訊是有道理的。

Except, they should only need to access it while helping with your reservation.

只是，他們應該只需要在協助你處理預訂時存取它。

To maintain privacy, security controls are intended to limit access based on the user and situation.

為了維護隱私，安全控制旨在根據使用者和情況限制存取。

This is known as the principle of least privilege.

這稱為最小權限原則。

Security controls should be designed with the principle of least privilege in mind.

安全控制的設計應以最小權限原則為考量。

When they are, they rely on differentiating between data owners and data custodians.

當它們是這樣設計時，它們依賴於區分資料擁有者和資料保管者。

A data owner is a person who decides who can access, edit, use, or destroy their information.

資料擁有者是決定誰可以存取、編輯、使用或銷毀其資訊的人。

The idea is very straightforward except in cases where there are multiple owners.

這個概念非常簡單，除非有多個所有者的情況。

For example, the intellectual property of an organization can have multiple data owners.

例如，組織的智慧財產權可能有多個資料擁有者。

A data custodian is anyone or anything that's responsible for the safe handling, transport, and storage of information.

資料保管者是任何負責資訊的安全處理、傳輸和儲存的人或物。

Did you notice that I mentioned, "anything?" That's because, aside from people, organizations and their systems are also custodians of people's information.

你有注意到我提到「任何物」嗎？那是因為，除了人以外，組織及其系統也是人們資訊的保管者。

There are other considerations besides these when implementing security controls.

在實施安全控制時，除了這些之外還有其他需要考慮的因素。

Remember that data is an asset.

請記住，資料是一種資產。

Like any other asset, information privacy requires proper classification and handling.

像任何其他資產一樣，資訊隱私需要適當的分類和處理。

As we progress in this section, we'll continue exploring other security controls that make this possible.

隨著我們在本節中的進展，我們將繼續探索其他使這一切成為可能的安全控制措施。

### Principle of least privilege 最小權限原則

Security controls are essential to keeping sensitive data private and safe.

安全控制對於保護敏感資料的隱私和安全至關重要。

One of the most common controls is the principle of least privilege, also referred to as PoLP or least privilege.

最常見的控制措施之一是最小權限原則，也稱為 PoLP 或 least privilege。

The principle of least privilege is a security concept in which a user is only granted the minimum level of access and authorization required to complete a task or function.

最小權限原則是一種安全概念，其中僅授予使用者完成任務或功能所需的最低存取權限和授權級別。

Least privilege is a fundamental security control that supports the confidentiality, integrity, and availability (CIA) triad of information.

最小權限是支援資訊的機密性、完整性和可用性 (CIA) 三要素的基本安全控制。

In this reading, you'll learn how the principle of least privilege reduces risk, how it's commonly implemented, and why it should be routinely audited.

在這篇閱讀中，你將學習最小權限原則如何降低風險、它通常如何實施，以及為什麼應該定期稽核它。

**Limiting access reduces risk**

**限制存取可降低風險**

Every business needs to plan for the risk of data theft, misuse, or abuse.

每個企業都需要規劃資料竊取、濫用或誤用的風險。

Implementing the principle of least privilege can greatly reduce the risk of costly incidents like data breaches by:

實施最小權限原則可以透過以下方式大大降低諸如資料外洩等代價高昂的事件的風險：

-   Limiting access to sensitive information
    限制對敏感資訊的存取
-   Reducing the chances of accidental data modification, tampering, or loss
    減少意外修改、竄改或遺失資料的機會
-   Supporting system monitoring and administration
    支援系統監控和管理

Least privilege greatly reduces the likelihood of a successful attack by connecting specific resources to specific users and placing limits on what they can do.

最小權限透過將特定資源連接到特定使用者並限制他們可以執行的操作，大大降低了成功攻擊的可能性。

It's an important security control that should be applied to any asset.

這是一個重要的安全控制，應應用於任何資產。

Clearly defining who or what your users are is usually the first step of implementing least privilege effectively.

清楚定義你的使用者是誰或什麼通常是有效實施最小權限的第一步。

Note: Least privilege is closely related to another fundamental security principle, the separation of duties—a security concept that divides tasks and responsibilities among different users to prevent giving a single user complete control over critical business functions.

注意：最小權限與另一個基本安全原則（職責分離）密切相關——職責分離是一種安全概念，它將任務和責任分配給不同的使用者，以防止單個使用者完全控制關鍵業務功能。

You'll learn more about separation of duties in a different reading about identity and access management.

你將在另一篇關於身分和存取管理的文章中學到更多關於職責分離的資訊。

**Determining access and authorization**

**確定存取和授權**

To implement least privilege, access and authorization must be determined first.

要實施最小權限，必須首先確定存取和授權。

There are two questions to ask to do so:
要做到這一點，需要問兩個問題：

-   Who is the user? 使用者是誰？
-   How much access do they need to a specific resource? 他們需要對特定資源有多少存取權限？

Determining who the user is usually straightforward.

確定使用者是誰通常很簡單。

A user can refer to a person, like a customer, an employee, or a vendor.

使用者可以指一個人，例如客戶、員工或供應商。

It can also refer to a device or software that's connected to your business network.

它也可以指連接到你企業網路的裝置或軟體。

In general, every user should have their own account.

一般來說，每個使用者都應該有自己的帳戶。

Accounts are typically stored and managed within an organization's directory service.

帳戶通常儲存在組織的目錄服務中並由其管理。

These are the most common types of user accounts:
以下是最常見的使用者帳戶類型：

-   Guest accounts are provided to external users who need to access an internal network, like customers, clients, contractors, or business partners.

    訪客帳戶提供給需要存取內部網路的外部使用者，例如客戶、委託人、承包商或業務合作夥伴。

-   User accounts are assigned to staff based on their job duties.

    使用者帳戶根據員工的職責分配給員工。

-   Service accounts are granted to applications or software that needs to interact with other software on the network.

    服務帳戶授予需要與網路上其他軟體互動的應用程式或軟體。

-   Privileged accounts have elevated permissions or administrative access.

    特權帳戶具有更高的權限或管理員存取權限。

It's best practice to determine a baseline access level for each account type before implementing least privilege.

最佳實務是在實施最小權限之前，先確定每種帳戶類型的基準存取級別。

However, the appropriate access level can change from one moment to the next.

但是，適當的存取級別可能會隨時變化。

For example, a customer support representative should only have access to your information while they are helping you.

例如，客戶支援代表應該只在他們幫助你時才能存取你的資訊。

Your data should then become inaccessible when the support agent starts working with another customer and they are no longer actively assisting you.

當支援專員開始與另一位客戶合作且不再主動協助你時，你的資料應該變得無法存取。

Least privilege can only reduce risk if user accounts are routinely and consistently monitored.

只有定期且持續地監控使用者帳戶，最小權限才能降低風險。

Pro tip: Passwords play an important role when implementing the principle of least privilege.

專家提示：密碼在實施最小權限原則時起著重要作用。

Even if user accounts are assigned appropriately, an insecure password can compromise your systems.

即使使用者帳戶分配得當，不安全的密碼也可能危及你的系統。

**Auditing account privileges**

**稽核帳戶權限**

Setting up the right user accounts and assigning them the appropriate privileges is a helpful first step.

設定正確的使用者帳戶並分配適當的權限是有幫助的第一步。

Periodically auditing those accounts is a key part of keeping your company’s systems secure.

定期稽核這些帳戶是保持公司系統安全的關鍵部分。

There are three common approaches to auditing user accounts:
有三種常見的稽核使用者帳戶方法：

-   Usage audits
-   使用情況稽核
-   Privilege audits
-   權限稽核
-   Account change audits
-   帳戶變更稽核

As a security professional, you might be involved with any of these processes.

作為一名安全專業人員，你可能會參與這些流程中的任何一個。

**Usage audits**

**使用情況稽核**

When conducting a usage audit, the security team will review which resources each account is accessing and what the user is doing with the resource.

在進行使用情況稽核時，安全團隊將審查每個帳戶正在存取哪些資源，以及使用者如何使用這些資源。

Usage audits can help determine whether users are acting in accordance with an organization’s security policies.

使用情況稽核可以幫助確定使用者是否按照組織的安全政策行事。

They can also help identify whether a user has permissions that can be revoked because they are no longer being used.

它們還可以幫助識別使用者是否擁有可以撤銷的權限，因為這些權限不再被使用。

**Privilege audits**

**權限稽核**

Users tend to accumulate more access privileges than they need over time, an issue known as privilege creep.

隨著時間的推移，使用者往往會累積比他們需要的更多的存取權限，這個問題稱為權限蔓延。

This might occur if an employee receives a promotion or switches teams and their job duties change.

如果員工獲得晉升或轉換團隊且其工作職責發生變化，則可能會發生這種情況。

Privilege audits assess whether a user's role is in alignment with the resources they have access to.

權限稽核評估使用者的角色是否與他們有權存取的資源一致。

**Account change audits**

**帳戶變更稽核**

Account directory services keep records and logs associated with each user.

帳戶目錄服務會保存與每個使用者相關的記錄和日誌。

Changes to an account are usually saved and can be used to audit the directory for suspicious activity, like multiple attempts to change an account password.

對帳戶的變更通常會被保存下來，可用於稽核目錄中是否有可疑活動，例如多次嘗試變更帳戶密碼。

Performing account change audits helps to ensure that all account changes are made by authorized users.

執行帳戶變更稽核有助於確保所有帳戶變更都是由授權使用者進行的。

Note: Most directory services can be configured to alert system administrators of suspicious activity.

注意：大多數目錄服務都可以配置為在發生可疑活動時向系統管理員發出警報。

**Key takeaways**

**重點回顧**

The principle of least privilege is a security control that can reduce the risk of unauthorized access to sensitive information and resources.

最小權限原則是一種安全控制，可以降低未經授權存取敏感資訊和資源的風險。

Setting up and configuring user accounts with the right levels of access and authorization is an important step toward implementing least privilege.

使用適當的存取和授權級別來設定和配置使用者帳戶是實施最小權限的重要一步。

Auditing user accounts and revoking unnecessary access rights is an important practice that helps to maintain the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information.

稽核使用者帳戶並撤銷不必要的存取權限是重要的實務，有助於維護資訊的機密性、完整性和可用性。

### The data lifecycle 資料生命週期

The data lifecycle
資料生命週期

Organizations of all sizes handle a large amount of data that must be kept private.

各種規模的組織都處理大量必須保密的資料。

You learned that data can be vulnerable whether it is at rest, in use, or in transit.

你學到無論資料處於靜止、使用中或傳輸中，都可能存在漏洞。

Regardless of the state it is in, information should be kept private by limiting access and authorization.

無論資訊處於何種狀態，都應透過限制存取和授權來保持其私密性。

In security, data vulnerabilities are often mapped in a model known as the data lifecycle.

在安全領域，資料漏洞通常以稱為資料生命週期的模型來繪製。

Each stage of the data lifecycle plays an important role in the security controls that are put in place to maintain the CIA triad of information.

資料生命週期的每個階段在為維護資訊的 CIA 三要素而實施的安全控制中都扮演著重要的角色。

In this reading, you will learn about the data lifecycle, the plans that determine how data is protected, and the specific types of data that require extra attention.

在這篇閱讀中，你將學習資料生命週期、決定如何保護資料的計畫，以及需要特別注意的特定資料類型。

**The data lifecycle**

**資料生命週期**

The data lifecycle is an important model that security teams consider when protecting information.

資料生命週期是安全團隊在保護資訊時會考慮的重要模型。

It influences how they set policies that align with business objectives.

它會影響他們如何制定符合業務目標的政策。

It also plays an important role in the technologies security teams use to make information accessible.

它在安全團隊用來使資訊可存取的技術中也扮演著重要的角色。

In general, the data lifecycle has five stages.

一般來說，資料生命週期有五個階段。

Each describe how data flows through an organization from the moment it is created until it is no longer useful:
每個階段都描述了資料從建立到不再有用時如何在組織中流動：

-   Collect 收集
-   Store 儲存
-   Use 使用
-   Archive 封存
-   Destroy 銷毀

Protecting information at each stage of this process describes the need to keep it accessible and recoverable should something go wrong.

在這個過程的每個階段保護資訊都說明了在發生問題時需要保持其可存取性和可恢復性。

**Data governance**

**資料治理**

Businesses handle massive amounts of data every day.

企業每天處理大量的資料。

New information is constantly being collected from internal and external sources.

不斷地從內部和外部來源收集新的資訊。

A structured approach to managing all of this data is the best way to keep it private and secure.

以結構化的方法管理所有這些資料是保持其私密性和安全性的最佳方式。

Data governance is a set of processes that define how an organization manages information.

資料治理是一組定義組織如何管理資訊的流程。

Governance often includes policies that specify how to keep data private, accurate, available, and secure throughout its lifecycle.

治理通常包括指定如何在資料的整個生命週期中保持其私密性、準確性、可用性和安全性的政策。

Effective data governance is a collaborative activity that relies on people.

有效的資料治理是一項依賴人員的協作活動。

Data governance policies commonly categorize individuals into a specific role:
資料治理政策通常將個人分為特定的角色：

-   Data owner: the person that decides who can access, edit, use, or destroy their information.

    資料擁有者：決定誰可以存取、編輯、使用或銷毀其資訊的人。

-   Data custodian: anyone or anything that's responsible for the safe handling, transport, and storage of information.

    資料保管者：任何負責資訊的安全處理、傳輸和儲存的人或物。

**Information security vs.information privacy**

安全和隱私是兩個經常在本領域之外互換使用的術語。

儘管這兩個概念是相關的，但它們代表特定的功能：

例如，如果使用者在網路上搜尋某個產品，公司便開始儲存並與其他公司分享有關該使用者搜尋記錄的資訊存取權。

然後，企業能夠免費向使用者提供個人化的購物體驗。

Eventually this practice led to a global conversation about whether these organizations had the right to collect and share someone’s private data.

最終，這種做法引發了全球性的討論，即這些組織是否有權收集和分享某人的私人資料。

此外，資料安全問題變得更加令人擔憂；組織收集的資料越多，就越容易受到濫用、誤用或竊取。

Many organizations became more concerned about the issues of data privacy.

許多組織開始更加關注資料隱私問題。

Businesses became more transparent about how they were collecting, storing, and using information.

企業開始更加透明地說明他們如何收集、儲存和使用資訊。

They also began implementing more security measures to protect people's data privacy.

他們也開始實施更多的安全措施來保護人們的資料隱私。

However, without clear rules in place, protections were inconsistently applied.

然而，在沒有明確規則的情況下，保護措施的應用並不一致。

Note: The more data is collected, stored, and used, the more vulnerable it is to breaches and threats.

注意：收集、儲存和使用的資料越多，就越容易受到外洩和威脅。

## Encryption methods 加密方法

### Fundamentals of cryptography 密碼學基礎

The internet is an open, public system with a lot of data flowing through it.

網際網路是一個開放的公共系統，其中有大量資料在流動。

Even though we all send and store information online, there's some information that we choose to keep private.

即使我們都在網路上發送和儲存資訊，但有些資訊我們選擇保密。

In security, this type of data is known as personally identifiable information.

在安全領域，這類資料稱為個人識別資訊。

Personally identifiable information, or PII, is any information that can be used to infer an individual's identity.

個人識別資訊 (PII) 是任何可用於推斷個人身分的資訊。

This can include things like someone's name, medical and financial information, photos, emails, or fingerprints.

這可能包括某人的姓名、醫療和財務資訊、照片、電子郵件或指紋等。

Maintaining the privacy of PII online is difficult.

在網路上維護 PII 的隱私很困難。

It takes the right security controls to do so.

這需要正確的安全控制措施才能做到。

One of the main security controls used to protect information online is cryptography.

用於在網路上保護資訊的主要安全控制措施之一是密碼學。

Cryptography is the process of transforming information into a form that unintended readers can't understand.

密碼學是將資訊轉換成非預期讀者無法理解的形式的過程。

Data of any kind is kept secret using a two-step process: encryption to hide the information, and decryption to unhide it.

任何類型的資料都使用一個兩步驟的過程來保密：加密以隱藏資訊，解密以取消隱藏資訊。

Imagine sending an email to a friend.

想像一下發送電子郵件給朋友。

The process starts by taking data in its original and readable form, known as plaintext.

該過程首先取得原始且可讀的資料形式，稱為明文。

Encryption takes that information and scrambles it into an unreadable form, known as ciphertext.

加密會取得該資訊並將其攪亂成無法讀取的形式，稱為密文。

We then use decryption to unscramble the ciphertext back into plaintext form, making it readable again.

然後我們使用解密將密文解碼回明文形式，使其再次可讀。

Hiding and unhiding private information is a practice that's been around for a long time.

隱藏和取消隱藏私人資訊的做法已經存在很長時間了。

Way before computers! One of the earliest cryptographic methods is known as Caesar's cipher.

遠在電腦發明之前！最早的密碼學方法之一稱為凱撒密碼。

This method is named after a Roman general, Julius Caesar, who ruled the Roman empire near the end of the first century BC.

此方法以羅馬將軍尤利烏斯·凱撒的名字命名，他於西元前一世紀末統治羅馬帝國。

He used it to keep messages between him and his military generals private.

他用它來保密他和他手下將軍之間的訊息。

Caesar's cipher is a pretty simple algorithm that works by shifting letters in the Roman alphabet forward by a fixed number of spaces.

凱撒密碼是一種非常簡單的演算法，其工作方式是將羅馬字母中的字母向前移動固定的空格數。

An algorithm is a set of rules that solve a problem.

演算法是一組解決問題的規則。

Specifically in cryptography, a cipher is an algorithm that encrypts information.

具體來說，在密碼學中，密碼是一種加密資訊的演算法。

For example, a message encoded with Caesar's cipher using a shift of 3 would encode an A as a D, a B as an E, a C as an F, and so on.

例如，使用凱撒密碼以 3 的位移編碼的訊息會將 A 編碼為 D，B 編碼為 E，C 編碼為 F，依此類推。

In this example, you could send a friend a message that said, "hello" using a shift of 3, and it would read "khoor."
在這個例子中，你可以使用 3 的位移發送一條訊息給朋友，內容是 "hello"，它會變成 "khoor"。

Now, you might be wondering how would you know the shift a message encrypted with Caesar's cipher is using.

現在，你可能想知道如何知道使用凱撒密碼加密的訊息使用的是哪個位移。

The answer to that is, you need the key!
答案是你需要金鑰！

A cryptographic key is a mechanism that decrypts ciphertext.

密碼金鑰是一種解密密文的機制。

In our example, the key would tell you that my message is encrypted by 3 shifts.

在我們的例子中，金鑰會告訴你我的訊息是用 3 個位移加密的。

With that information, you can unlock the hidden message!
有了這些資訊，你就可以解鎖隱藏的訊息！

Every form of encryption relies on both a cipher and key to secure the exchange of information.

每種形式的加密都依賴於密碼和金鑰來確保資訊交換的安全。

Caesar's cipher is not widely used today because of a couple of major flaws.

由於一些主要缺陷，凱撒密碼現在沒有被廣泛使用。

One concerns the cipher itself.

一個與密碼本身有關。

The other relates to the key.

另一個與金鑰有關。

This particular cipher relies entirely on the characters of the Roman alphabet to hide information.

這種特殊的密碼完全依賴羅馬字母的字元來隱藏資訊。

For example, consider a message written using the English alphabet, which is only 26 characters.

例如，考慮使用英文字母寫的訊息，它只有 26 個字元。

Even without the key, it's pretty simple to crack a message secured with Caesar's cipher by shifting letters 26 different ways.

即使沒有金鑰，透過 26 種不同的方式移動字母，也很容易破解用凱撒密碼保護的訊息。

In information security, this tactic is known as brute force attack, a trial-and-error process of discovering private information.

在資訊安全中，這種策略稱為暴力破解攻擊，是一種透過反覆試驗來發現私人資訊的過程。

The other major flaw of Caesar's cipher is that it relies on a single key.

凱撒密碼的另一個主要缺陷是它依賴單個金鑰。

If that key was lost or stolen, there's nothing stopping someone from accessing private information.

如果該金鑰遺失或被盜，就沒有什麼可以阻止某人存取私人資訊。

Properly keeping track of cryptographic keys is an important part of security.

妥善追蹤密碼金鑰是安全的重要組成部分。

To start, it's important to ensure that these keys are not stored in public places, and to share them separately from the information they will decrypt.

首先，重要的是要確保這些金鑰不儲存在公共場所，並將它們與它們將解密的資訊分開共享。

Caesar's cipher is just one of many algorithms used to protect people's privacy.

凱撒密碼只是用於保護人們隱私的眾多演算法之一。

Due to its limitations, we rely on more complex algorithms to secure information online.

由於其局限性，我們依賴更複雜的演算法來保護線上資訊。

Our next focus is exploring how modern algorithms work to keep information private.

我們的下一個重點是探索現代演算法如何運作以保持資訊私密。

### Public key infrastructure 公開金鑰基礎建設

Computers use a lot of encryption algorithms to send and store information online.

電腦使用許多加密演算法來線上傳送和儲存資訊。

They're all helpful when it comes to hiding private information, but only as long as their keys are protected.

它們在隱藏私人資訊方面都很有用，但前提是它們的金鑰受到保護。

Can you imagine having to keep track of the encryption keys protecting all of your personal information online? Neither can I, and we don't have to, thanks to something known as public key infrastructure.

你能想像必須追蹤保護你所有線上個人資訊的加密金鑰嗎？我也無法想像，而且我們不必這樣做，這要歸功於一種稱為公開金鑰基礎建設的東西。

Public key infrastructure, or PKI, is an encryption framework that secures the exchange of information online.

公開金鑰基礎建設 (PKI) 是一種保護線上資訊交換的加密框架。

It's a broad system that makes accessing information fast, easy, and secure.

它是一個廣泛的系統，使存取資訊變得快速、容易且安全。

So, how does it all work?
那麼，這一切是如何運作的呢？

PKI is a two-step process.

PKI 是一個兩步驟的過程。

It all starts with the exchange of encrypted information.

這一切都始於加密資訊的交換。

This involves either asymmetric encryption, symmetric encryption, or both.

這涉及非對稱加密、對稱加密或兩者兼而有之。

Asymmetric encryption involves the use of a public and private key pair for encryption and decryption of data.

非對稱加密涉及使用一對公開金鑰和私密金鑰來加密和解密資料。

Let's imagine this as a box that can be opened with two keys.

讓我們將其想像成一個可以用兩把鑰匙打開的盒子。

One key, the public key, can only be used to access the slot and add items to the box.

其中一把鑰匙，即公開金鑰，只能用於存取插槽並將物品添加到盒子中。

Since the public key can't be used to remove items, it can be copied and shared with people all around the world to add items.

由於公開金鑰不能用於取出物品，因此可以將其複製並與世界各地的人們共享以添加物品。

On the other hand, the second key, the private key, opens the box fully, so that the items inside can be removed.

另一方面，第二把鑰匙，即私密金鑰，可以完全打開盒子，以便可以取出裡面的物品。

Only the owner of the box has access to the private key that unlocks it.

只有盒子所有者才能存取解鎖它的私密金鑰。

Using a public key allows the people and servers you're communicating with to see and send you encrypted information that only you can decrypt with your private key.

使用公開金鑰允許你正在與之通訊的人員和伺服器查看並向你傳送只有你可以使用你的私密金鑰解密的加密資訊。

This two-key system makes asymmetric encryption a secure way to exchange information online; however, it also slows down the process.

這種雙金鑰系統使非對稱加密成為在線上交換資訊的安全方式；但是，它也減慢了該過程。

Symmetric encryption, on the other hand, is a faster and simpler approach to key management.

另一方面，對稱加密是一種更快、更簡單的金鑰管理方法。

Symmetric encryption involves the use of a single secret key to exchange information.

對稱加密涉及使用單個密鑰來交換資訊。

Let's imagine the locked box again.

讓我們再次想像一下鎖上的盒子。

Instead of two keys, symmetric encryption uses the same key.

對稱加密使用相同的金鑰，而不是兩把鑰匙。

The owner can use it to open the box, add items, and close it again.

所有者可以使用它來打開盒子、添加物品，然後再次關閉它。

When they want to share access, they can give the secret key to anyone else to do the same.

當他們想要共享存取權限時，他們可以將密鑰提供給任何其他人以執行相同的操作。

Exchanging a single secret key may make web communications faster, but it also makes it less secure.

交換單個密鑰可能會使網路通訊更快，但也使其安全性降低。

PKI uses both asymmetric and symmetric encryption, sometimes in conjunction with one another.

PKI 同時使用非對稱加密和對稱加密，有時會結合使用。

It all depends on whether speed or security is the priority.

這一切都取決於速度還是安全性是優先考量。

For example, mobile chat applications use asymmetric encryption to establish a connection between people at the start of a conversation when security is the priority.

例如，行動聊天應用程式在對話開始時（當安全性是優先考量時）使用非對稱加密來建立人與人之間的連線。

Afterwards, when the speed of communications back-and-forth is the priority, symmetric encryption takes over.

之後，當來回通訊的速度是優先考量時，對稱加密就會接管。

While both have their own strengths and weaknesses, they share a common vulnerability, establishing trust between the sender and receiver.

雖然兩者都有各自的優缺點，但它們有一個共同的弱點，即在傳送者和接收者之間建立信任。

Both processes rely on sharing keys that can be misused, lost, or stolen.

這兩個過程都依賴於可能被誤用、遺失或被盜的金鑰共享。

This isn't a problem when we exchange information in person because we can use our senses to tell the difference between those we trust and those we don't trust.

當我們親自交換資訊時，這不是問題，因為我們可以利用我們的感官來分辨我們信任的人和我們不信任的人。

Computers, on the other hand, aren't naturally equipped to make this distinction.

另一方面，電腦天生不具備做出這種區分的能力。

That's where the second step of PKI applies.

這就是 PKI 的第二步應用之處。

PKI addresses the vulnerability of key sharing by establishing trust using a system of digital certificates between computers and networks.

PKI 透過在電腦和網路之間使用數位憑證系統來建立信任，從而解決了金鑰共享的弱點。

A digital certificate is a file that verifies the identity of a public key holder.

數位憑證是一個驗證公開金鑰持有者身分的檔案。

Most online information is exchanged using digital certificates.

大多數線上資訊都是使用數位憑證交換的。

Users, companies, and networks hold one and exchange them when communicating information online as a way of signaling trust.

使用者、公司和網路都持有一個數位憑證，並在線上通訊資訊時交換它們，以此作為表示信任的方式。

Let's look at an example of how digital certificates are created.

讓我們看一個如何建立數位憑證的範例。

Let's say an online business is about to launch their website, and they want to obtain a digital certificate.

假設一家線上企業即將推出他們的網站，他們想要獲得數位憑證。

When they register their domain, the hosting company sends certain information over to a trusted certificate authority, or CA.

當他們註冊網域時，託管公司會將某些資訊傳送給受信任的憑證授權單位 (CA)。

The information provided is usually basic things like the company name and the country where its headquarters are located.

提供的資訊通常是基本資訊，例如公司名稱及其總部所在的國家。

A public key for the site is also provided.

也會提供該網站的公開金鑰。

The certificate authority then uses this data to verify the company's identity.

然後，憑證授權單位使用這些資料來驗證公司的身分。

When it's confirmed, the CA encrypts the data with its own private key.

確認後，CA 會使用其自己的私密金鑰加密資料。

Finally, they create a digital certificate that contains the encrypted company data.

最後，他們建立一個包含加密公司資料的數位憑證。

It also contains CA's digital signature to prove that it's authentic.

它還包含 CA 的數位簽章，以證明其真實性。

Digital certificates are a lot like a digital ID badge that's used online to restrict or grant access to information.

數位憑證很像在線上使用的數位身分證，用於限制或授予資訊存取權限。

This is how PKI solves the trust issue.

這就是 PKI 如何解決信任問題的。

Combined with asymmetric and symmetric encryption, this two-step approach to exchanging secure information between trusted sources is what makes PKI such a useful security control.

結合非對稱加密和對稱加密，這種在受信任來源之間交換安全資訊的兩步驟方法使 PKI 成為如此有用的安全控制措施。

### Symmetric and asymmetric encryption 對稱與非對稱加密

Previously, you learned these terms:

先前，你學過這些術語：

-   Encryption: the process of converting data from a readable format to an encoded format
    加密：將資料從可讀格式轉換為編碼格式的過程
-   Public key infrastructure (PKI): an encryption framework that secures the exchange of online information
    公開金鑰基礎建設 (PKI)：保護線上資訊交換的加密框架
-   Cipher: an algorithm that encrypts information
    密碼：一種加密資訊的演算法

All digital information deserves to be kept private, safe, and secure.

所有數位資訊都應該保持私密、安全和受到保護。

Encryption is one key to doing that! It is useful for transforming information into a form that unintended recipients cannot understand.

加密是做到這一點的關鍵之一！它可用於將資訊轉換成非預期接收者無法理解的形式。

In this reading, you’ll compare symmetric and asymmetric encryption and learn about some well-known algorithms for each.

在這篇閱讀中，你將比較對稱和非對稱加密，並了解每種加密的一些著名演算法。

**Types of encryption**

**加密類型**

There are two main types of encryption:
主要有兩種類型的加密：

-   Symmetric encryption is the use of a single secret key to exchange information.

    對稱加密是使用單個密鑰來交換資訊。
    Because it uses one key for encryption and decryption, the sender and receiver must know the secret key to lock or unlock the cipher.

    由於它使用一個金鑰進行加密和解密，因此傳送者和接收者都必須知道密鑰才能鎖定或解鎖密碼。

-   Asymmetric encryption is the use of a public and private key pair for encryption and decryption of data.

    非對稱加密是使用一對公開金鑰和私密金鑰來加密和解密資料。
    It uses two separate keys: a public key and a private key.

    它使用兩個單獨的金鑰：一個公開金鑰和一個私密金鑰。
    The public key is used to encrypt data, and the private key decrypts it.

    公開金鑰用於加密資料，私密金鑰用於解密資料。
    The private key is only given to users with authorized access.

    私密金鑰僅提供給具有授權存取權限的使用者。

**The importance of key length**

**金鑰長度的重要性**

Ciphers are vulnerable to brute force attacks, which use a trial and error process to discover private information.

密碼容易受到暴力破解攻擊，這種攻擊使用反覆試驗的過程來發現私人資訊。

This tactic is the digital equivalent of trying every number in a combination lock trying to find the right one.

這種策略相當於數位化的嘗試密碼鎖中的每個數字以找到正確的數字。

In modern encryption, longer key lengths are considered to be more secure.

在現代加密中，較長的金鑰長度被認為更安全。

Longer key lengths mean more possibilities that an attacker needs to try to unlock a cipher.

較長的金鑰長度意味著攻擊者需要嘗試更多可能性才能解鎖密碼。

One drawback to having long encryption keys is slower processing times.

擁有長加密金鑰的一個缺點是處理時間較慢。

Although short key lengths are generally less secure, they’re much faster to compute.

雖然短金鑰長度通常不太安全，但它們的計算速度要快得多。

Providing fast data communication online while keeping information safe is a delicate balancing act.

在保持資訊安全的同時提供快速的線上資料通訊是一項微妙的平衡措施。

**Approved algorithms**

**核准的演算法**

Many web applications use a combination of symmetric and asymmetric encryption.

許多網路應用程式結合使用對稱和非對稱加密。

This is how they balance user experience with safeguarding information.

這就是它們如何在使用者體驗和保護資訊之間取得平衡的方式。

As an analyst, you should be aware of the most widely-used algorithms.

作為一名分析師，你應該了解最廣泛使用的演算法。

**Symmetric algorithms**

**對稱演算法**

-   Triple DES (3DES) is known as a block cipher because of the way it converts plaintext into ciphertext in “blocks.” Its origins trace back to the Data Encryption Standard (DES), which was developed in the early 1970s.

    三重 DES (3DES) 因其以「區塊」方式將明文轉換為密文的方式而聞名，稱為區塊密碼。它的起源可以追溯到 1970 年代初開發的資料加密標準 (DES)。
    DES was one of the earliest symmetric encryption algorithms that generated 64-bit keys, although only 56 bits are used for encryption.

    DES 是最早的對稱加密演算法之一，它產生 64 位元金鑰，但只有 56 位元用於加密。
    A bit is the smallest unit of data measurement on a computer.

    位元是電腦上最小的資料測量單位。
    As you might imagine, Triple DES generates keys that are three times as long.

    你可以想像，三重 DES 產生的金鑰長度是三倍。
    Triple DES applies the DES algorithm three times, using three different 56-bit keys.

    三重 DES 使用三個不同的 56 位元金鑰三次應用 DES 演算法。
    This results in an effective key length of 168 bits.

    這會產生 168 位元的有效金鑰長度。
    Despite the longer keys, many organizations are moving away from using Triple DES due to limitations on the amount of data that can be encrypted.

    儘管金鑰較長，但由於可加密的資料量有限制，許多組織正逐漸不再使用三重 DES。
    However, Triple DES is likely to remain in use for backwards compatibility purposes.

    但是，出於向後相容性的目的，三重 DES 可能會繼續使用。

-   Advanced Encryption Standard (AES) is one of the most secure symmetric algorithms today.

    進階加密標準 (AES) 是當今最安全的對稱演算法之一。
    AES generates keys that are 128, 192, or 256 bits.

    AES 產生的金鑰為 128、192 或 256 位元。
    Cryptographic keys of this size are considered to be safe from brute force attacks.

    這種大小的密碼金鑰被認為可以安全地抵禦暴力破解攻擊。
    It’s estimated that brute forcing an AES 128-bit key could take a modern computer billions of years!
    據估計，使用現代電腦暴力破解 AES 128 位元金鑰可能需要數十億年！

**Asymmetric algorithms**

**非對稱演算法**

Rivest Shamir Adleman (RSA) is named after its three creators who developed it while at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT).

Rivest Shamir Adleman (RSA) 以其三位在麻省理工學院 (MIT) 開發它的創作者的名字命名。

RSA is one of the first asymmetric encryption algorithms that produces a public and private key pair.

RSA 是最早產生公開金鑰和私密金鑰配對的非對稱加密演算法之一。

Asymmetric algorithms like RSA produce even longer key lengths.

像 RSA 這樣的非對稱演算法產生更長的金鑰長度。

In part, this is due to the fact that these functions are creating two keys.

部分原因是這些函數正在建立兩個金鑰。

RSA key sizes are 1,024, 2,048, or 4,096 bits.

RSA 金鑰大小為 1,024、2,048 或 4,096 位元。

RSA is mainly used to protect highly sensitive data.

RSA 主要用於保護高度敏感的資料。

Digital Signature Algorithm (DSA) is a standard asymmetric algorithm that was introduced by NIST in the early 1990s.

數位簽章演算法 (DSA) 是 NIST 在 1990 年代初推出的一種標準非對稱演算法。

DSA also generates key lengths of 2,048 bits.

DSA 也產生 2,048 位元的金鑰長度。

This algorithm is widely used today as a complement to RSA in public key infrastructure.

此演算法如今廣泛用作公開金鑰基礎建設中 RSA 的補充。

**Generating keys**

**產生金鑰**

These algorithms must be implemented when an organization chooses one to protect their data.

當組織選擇一種演算法來保護其資料時，必須實施這些演算法。

One way this is done is using OpenSSL, which is an open-source command line tool that can be used to generate public and private keys.

一種方法是使用 OpenSSL，這是一個可用於產生公開金鑰和私密金鑰的開放原始碼命令列工具。

OpenSSL is commonly used by computers to verify digital certificates that are exchanged as part of public key infrastructure.

電腦通常使用 OpenSSL 來驗證作為公開金鑰基礎建設一部分交換的數位憑證。

Note: OpenSSL is just one option.

注意：OpenSSL 只是一種選擇。

There are various others available that can generate keys with any of these common algorithms.

還有其他各種可用的工具可以使用任何這些常見的演算法來產生金鑰。

In early 2014, OpenSSL disclosed a vulnerability, known as the "Heartbleed bug", that exposed sensitive data in the memory of websites and applications.

2014 年初，OpenSSL 披露了一個漏洞，稱為「Heartbleed 漏洞」，該漏洞洩露了網站和應用程式記憶體中的敏感資料。

Although unpatched versions of OpenSSL are still available, the Heartbleed bug was patched later that year (2014).

雖然未修補版本的 OpenSSL 仍然可用，但 Heartbleed 漏洞已於當年 (2014 年) 稍後進行了修補。

Many businesses today use the secure versions of OpenSSL to generate public and private keys, demonstrating the importance of using up-to-date software.

如今，許多企業使用安全版本的 OpenSSL 來產生公開金鑰和私密金鑰，這展現了使用最新軟體的重要性。

**Obscurity is not security**

**隱蔽性不是安全性**

In the world of cryptography, a cipher must be proven to be unbreakable before claiming that it is secure.

在密碼學的世界中，必須先證明密碼是無法破解的，然後才能聲稱它是安全的。

According to "Kerckhoff’s principle", cryptography should be designed in such a way that all the details of an algorithm—except for the private key—should be knowable without sacrificing its security.

根據「克爾克霍夫原則」，密碼學的設計方式應使演算法的所有細節（私密金鑰除外）都應該是可知的，而不會犧牲其安全性。

For example, you can access all the details about how AES encryption works online and yet it is still unbreakable.

例如，你可以在線上存取有關 AES 加密如何運作的所有細節，但它仍然是無法破解的。

Occasionally, organizations implement their own, custom encryption algorithms.

有時，組織會實施自己客製化的加密演算法。

There have been instances where those secret cryptographic systems have been quickly cracked after being made public.

曾有過一些案例，這些秘密的密碼系統在公開後很快就被破解了。

Pro tip: A cryptographic system should not be considered secure if it requires secrecy around how it works.

專家提示：如果一個密碼系統需要對其運作方式保密，則不應認為它是安全的。

**Encryption is everywhere**

**加密無處不在**

Companies use both symmetric and asymmetric encryption.

公司同時使用對稱和非對稱加密。

They often work as a team, balancing security with user experience.

它們通常協同工作，在安全性和使用者體驗之間取得平衡。

For example, websites tend to use asymmetric encryption to secure small blocks of data that are important.

例如，網站傾向於使用非對稱加密來保護重要的小區塊資料。

Usernames and passwords are often secured with asymmetric encryption while processing login requests.

使用者名稱和密碼通常在使用非對稱加密處理登入請求時受到保護。

Once a user gains access, the rest of their web session often switches to using symmetric encryption for its speed.

一旦使用者獲得存取權限，他們網路連線的其餘部分通常會切換到使用對稱加密以提高速度。

Using data encryption like this is increasingly required by law.

法律越來越要求使用這種資料加密方式。

Regulations like the Federal Information Processing Standards (FIPS 140-3) and the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) outline how data should be collected, used, and handled.

聯邦資訊處理標準 (FIPS 140-3) 和通用資料保護規則 (GDPR) 等法規概述了應如何收集、使用和處理資料。

Achieving compliance with either regulation is critical to demonstrating to business partners and governments that customer data is handled responsibly.

要向業務合作夥伴和政府證明客戶資料得到負責任的處理，符合任何一項法規都至關重要。

**Key takeaways**

**重點回顧**

Knowing the basics of encryption is important for all security professionals.

了解加密的基礎知識對於所有安全專業人員都很重要。

Symmetric encryption relies on a single secret key to protect data.

對稱加密依賴單個密鑰來保護資料。

On the other hand, asymmetric uses a public and private key pair.

另一方面，非對稱加密使用一對公開金鑰和私密金鑰。

Their encryption algorithms create different key sizes.

它們的加密演算法會建立不同的金鑰大小。

Both types of encryption are used to meet compliance regulations and protect data online.

這兩種類型的加密都用於符合合規法規並保護線上資料。

### Non-repudiation and hashing 不可否認性和雜湊

Security professionals are always thinking about vulnerabilities.

安全專業人員總是在思考漏洞。

It's how we stay ahead of threats.

這是我們保持領先於威脅的方式。

We've spent some time together exploring a couple forms of encryption.

我們花了一些時間一起探索了幾種加密形式。

The two types we've discussed produce keys that are shared when communicating information.

我們討論的兩種型別會產生在通訊資訊時共享的金鑰。

Encryption keys are vulnerable to being lost or stolen, which can lead to sensitive information at risk.

加密金鑰容易遺失或被盜，這可能導致敏感資訊面臨風險。

Let's explore another security control that helps companies address this weakness.

讓我們探索另一種有助於公司應對此弱點的安全控制措施。

A hash function is an algorithm that produces a code that can't be decrypted.

雜湊函數是一種產生無法解密的程式碼的演算法。

Unlike asymmetric and symmetric algorithms, hash functions are one-way processes that do not generate decryption keys.

與非對稱和對稱演算法不同，雜湊函數是單向過程，不會產生解密金鑰。

Instead, these algorithms produce a unique identifier known as a hash value, or digest.

相反地，這些演算法會產生一個稱為雜湊值或摘要的唯一識別碼。

Here's an example to demonstrate this.

這裡有一個例子來示範這一點。

Imagine a company has an internal application that is used by employees and is stored in a shared drive.

想像一下，一家公司有一個供員工使用的內部應用程式，並儲存在共用磁碟機中。

After passing through a hashing function, the program receives its hash value.

在通過雜湊函數後，該程式會收到其雜湊值。

For example purposes, we created this relatively short hash value with the MD5 hashing function.

為了舉例說明，我們使用 MD5 雜湊函數建立了一個相對較短的雜湊值。

Generally, standard hash functions that produce longer hashes are preferred for being more secure.

通常，產生較長雜湊的標準雜湊函數因更安全而受到青睞。

Next, let's imagine an attacker replaces the program with a modified version that performs malicious actions.

接下來，讓我們想像一個攻擊者用一個執行惡意操作的修改版本替換了該程式。

The malicious program may work like the original.

惡意程式可能像原始程式一樣運作。

However, if so much as one line of code is different from the original, it will produce a different hash value.

但是，如果哪怕只有一行程式碼與原始程式碼不同，它也會產生不同的雜湊值。

By comparing the hash values, we can validate that the programs are different.

透過比較雜湊值，我們可以驗證這些程式是不同的。

Attackers use tricks like this often because they're easily overlooked.

攻擊者經常使用這樣的技巧，因為它們很容易被忽略。

Fortunately, hash values help us identify when something like this is happening.

幸運的是，雜湊值可以幫助我們識別何時發生類似情況。

In security, hashes are primarily used as a way to determine the integrity of files and applications.

在安全領域，雜湊主要用作判斷檔案和應用程式完整性的一種方式。

Data integrity relates to the accuracy and consistency of information.

資料完整性與資訊的準確性和一致性有關。

This is known as non-repudiation, the concept that authenticity of information can't be denied.

這稱為不可否認性，即資訊的真實性不能被否認的概念。

Hash functions are important security controls that make proven data integrity possible.

雜湊函數是重要的安全控制措施，可實現經證實的資料完整性。

Analysts use them frequently.

分析師經常使用它們。

One way to do this is by finding the hash value of files or applications and comparing them against known malicious files.

一種方法是找到檔案或應用程式的雜湊值，並將它們與已知的惡意檔案進行比較。

For example, we can use the Linux command line to generate the hash value for any file on your computer.

例如，我們可以使用 Linux 命令列來產生電腦上任何檔案的雜湊值。

We just launch a shell and type the name of the hashing algorithm we want to use.

我們只需啟動一個 shell 並輸入我們要使用的雜湊演算法的名稱。

In this case, we're using a common one known as sha256.

在本例中，我們使用的是一個稱為 sha256 的常見演算法。

Next, we need to enter the file name of any file we want to hash.

接下來，我們需要輸入我們要雜湊的任何檔案的檔案名稱。

Let's hash the contents of newfile.txt.

讓我們雜湊 newfile.txt 的內容。

Now, we'll press Enter.

現在，我們按下 Enter 鍵。

The terminal generates this unique hash value for the file.

終端機為該檔案產生這個唯一的雜湊值。

These tools can be compared with the hash values of known online viruses.

這些工具可以與已知線上病毒的雜湊值進行比較。

One such database is VirusTotal.

其中一個這樣的資料庫是 VirusTotal。

This is a popular tool among security practitioners that's useful for analyzing suspicious files, domains, IPs, and URLs.

這是安全從業人員中流行的工具，可用於分析可疑檔案、網域、IP 和 URL。

As we've explored, even the slightest change in input results in a totally different hash value.

正如我們所探討的，即使輸入發生最輕微的變化，也會產生完全不同的雜湊值。

Hash functions are intentionally designed this way to assist with matters of non-repudiation.

雜湊函數經過刻意設計成這樣，以協助處理不可否認性的問題。

They equip computers with a quick and easy way to compare input and output values and validate data integrity.

它們為電腦配備了一種快速簡便的方法來比較輸入和輸出值並驗證資料完整性。

Pretty cool, right?
很酷，對吧？

### The evolution of hash functions 雜湊函數的演進

Hash functions are important controls that are part of every company's security strategy.

雜湊函數是每個公司安全策略的重要組成部分。

Hashing is widely used for authentication and non-repudiation, the concept that the authenticity of information can’t be denied.

雜湊廣泛用於身份驗證和不可否認性，即資訊的真實性不能被否認的概念。

Previously, you learned that hash functions are algorithms that produce a code that can't be decrypted.

先前，你學到雜湊函數是產生無法解密的程式碼的演算法。

Hash functions convert information into a unique value that can then be used to determine its integrity.

雜湊函數將資訊轉換為唯一值，然後可用於判斷其完整性。

In this reading, you’ll learn about the origins of hash functions and how they’ve changed over time.

在這篇閱讀中，你將了解雜湊函數的起源以及它們如何隨著時間而變化。

**Origins of hashing**

**雜湊的起源**

Hash functions have been around since the early days of computing.

雜湊函數自電腦發展初期就已存在。

They were originally created as a way to quickly search for data.

它們最初是作為一種快速搜尋資料的方式而建立的。

Since the beginning, these algorithms have been designed to represent data of any size as small, fixed-size values, or digests.

從一開始，這些演算法就被設計成將任何大小的資料表示為小的、固定大小的值或摘要。

Using a hash table, which is a data structure that's used to store and reference hash values, these small values became a more secure and efficient way for computers to reference data.

使用雜湊表（一種用於儲存和參考雜湊值的資料結構），這些小值成為電腦參考資料的更安全、更有效的方式。

One of the earliest hash functions is Message Digest 5, more commonly known as MD5.

最早的雜湊函數之一是訊息摘要 5，更廣為人知的是 MD5。

Professor Ronald Rivest of the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) developed MD5 in the early 1990s as a way to verify that a file sent over a network matched its source file.

麻省理工學院 (MIT) 的 Ronald Rivest 教授在 1990 年代初開發了 MD5，作為一種驗證透過網路傳送的檔案是否與其來源檔案匹配的方法。

Whether it’s used to convert a single email or the source code of an application, MD5 works by converting data into a 128-bit value.

無論是用於轉換單封電子郵件還是應用程式的原始碼，MD5 的工作方式都是將資料轉換為 128 位元的值。

You might recall that a bit is the smallest unit of data measurement on a computer.

你可能還記得，位元是電腦上最小的資料測量單位。

Bits can either be a 0 or 1.

位元可以是 0 或 1。

In a computer, bits represent user input in a way that computers can interpret.

在電腦中，位元以電腦可以理解的方式表示使用者輸入。

In a hash table, this appears as a string of 32 characters.

在雜湊表中，這顯示為 32 個字元的字串。

Altering anything in the source file generates an entirely new hash value.

更改來源檔案中的任何內容都會產生一個全新的雜湊值。

Generally, the longer the hash value, the more secure it is.

通常，雜湊值越長，就越安全。

It wasn’t long after MD5's creation that security practitioners discovered 128-bit digests resulted in a major vulnerability.

MD5 建立後不久，安全從業人員就發現 128 位元的摘要會導致嚴重的漏洞。

以下是如何將明文轉換為雜湊值的範例：

**Hash collisions**

**雜湊碰撞**

One of the flaws in MD5 happens to be a characteristic of all hash functions.

MD5 的缺陷之一恰好是所有雜湊函數的共同特徵。

Hash algorithms map any input, regardless of its length, into a fixed-size value of letters and numbers.

雜湊演算法將任何輸入（無論其長度如何）對應到固定大小的字母和數字值。

What’s the problem with that? Although there are an infinite amount of possible inputs, there’s only a finite set of available outputs!
這有什麼問題？雖然可能有無限多的輸入，但只有有限組可用的輸出！

MD5 values are limited to 32 characters in length.

MD5 值長度限制為 32 個字元。

Due to the limited output size, the algorithm is considered to be vulnerable to hash collision, an instance when different inputs produce the same hash value.

由於有限的輸出大小，該演算法被認為容易受到雜湊碰撞的影響，雜湊碰撞是指不同的輸入產生相同的雜湊值的情況。

Because hashes are used for authentication, a hash collision is similar to copying someone’s identity.

由於雜湊用於身份驗證，因此雜湊碰撞類似於複製某人的身分。

Attackers can carry out collision attacks to fraudulently impersonate authentic data.

攻擊者可以進行碰撞攻擊，以欺詐性地冒充真實資料。

**Next-generation hashing**

**下一代雜湊**

To avoid the risk of hash collisions, functions that generated longer values were needed.

為了避免雜湊碰撞的風險，需要產生更長數值的函數。

MD5's shortcomings gave way to a new group of functions known as the Secure Hashing Algorithms, or SHAs.

MD5 的缺點讓位給了一組稱為安全雜湊演算法 (SHA) 的新函數。

The National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) approves each of these algorithms.

美國國家標準與技術研究院 (NIST) 核准了這些演算法中的每一個。

Numbers besides each SHA function indicate the size of its hash value in bits.

每個 SHA 函數旁邊的數字表示其雜湊值的位元大小。

Except for SHA-1, which produces a 160-bit digest, these algorithms are considered to be collision-resistant.

除了產生 160 位元摘要的 SHA-1 之外，這些演算法都被認為具有抗碰撞性。

However, that doesn’t make them invulnerable to other exploits.

然而，這並不能使它們免受其他漏洞利用的影響。

**Five functions make up the SHA family of algorithms:**

**構成 SHA 演算法系列的五個函數：**

-   SHA-1
-   SHA-224
-   SHA-256
-   SHA-384
-   SHA-512

**Secure password storage**

**安全密碼儲存**

Passwords are typically stored in a database where they are mapped to a username.

密碼通常儲存在資料庫中，並對應到一個使用者名稱。

The server receives a request for authentication that contains the credentials supplied by the user.

伺服器收到一個包含使用者提供的憑證的驗證請求。

It then looks up the username in the database and compares it with the password that was provided and verifies that it matches before granting them access.

然後它會在資料庫中查找使用者名稱，並將其與提供的密碼進行比較，驗證它們是否匹配，然後才授予他們存取權限。

This is a safe system unless an attacker gains access to the user database.

除非攻擊者獲得使用者資料庫的存取權限，否則這是一個安全的系統。

If passwords are stored in plaintext, then an attacker can steal that information and use it to access company resources.

如果密碼以明文形式儲存，則攻擊者可以竊取該資訊並使用它來存取公司資源。

Hashing adds an additional layer of security.

雜湊增加了額外的安全層。

Because hash values can't be reversed, an attacker would not be able to steal someone's login credentials if they managed to gain access to the database.

由於雜湊值無法反轉，因此即使攻擊者設法獲得資料庫的存取權限，也無法竊取某人的登入憑證。

**Rainbow tables**

**彩虹表**

A rainbow table is a file of pre-generated hash values and their associated plaintext.

彩虹表是一個包含預先產生的雜湊值及其相關明文的檔案。

They’re like dictionaries of weak passwords.

它們就像弱密碼的字典。

Attackers capable of obtaining an organization’s password database can use a rainbow table to compare them against all possible values.

能夠獲得組織密碼資料庫的攻擊者可以使用彩虹表將其與所有可能的值進行比較。

**Adding some “salt”**

**添加一些「鹽」**

Functions with larger digests are less vulnerable to collision and rainbow table attacks.

具有較大摘要的函數比較不容易受到碰撞和彩虹表攻擊。

But as you’re learning, no security control is perfect.

但正如你所學到的，沒有任何安全控制是完美的。

Salting is an additional safeguard that's used to strengthen hash functions.

加鹽是用於增強雜湊函數的額外安全措施。

A salt is a random string of characters that's added to data before it's hashed.

鹽是在資料雜湊之前添加到資料中的隨機字元串。

The additional characters produce a more unique hash value, making salted data resilient to rainbow table attacks.

額外的字元會產生更獨特的雜湊值，使加鹽的資料能夠抵抗彩虹表攻擊。

For example, a database containing passwords might have several hashed entries for the password "password." If those passwords were all salted, each entry would be completely different.

例如，包含密碼的資料庫可能有多個密碼「password」的雜湊條目。如果這些密碼都加了鹽，則每個條目都會完全不同。

That means an attacker using a rainbow table would be unable to find matching values for "password" in the database.

這表示使用彩虹表的攻擊者將無法在資料庫中找到與「password」匹配的值。

User input entering a hash function.

使用者輸入進入雜湊函數。

A random set of characters are added to the hashing process.

在雜湊過程中添加了一組隨機字元。

For this reason, salting has become increasingly common when storing passwords and other types of sensitive data.

因此，加鹽在儲存密碼和其他類型的敏感資料時變得越來越普遍。

The length and uniqueness of a salt is important.

鹽的長度和唯一性非常重要。

Similar to hash values, the longer and more complex a salt is, the harder it is to crack.

與雜湊值類似，鹽越長、越複雜，就越難破解。

**Key takeaways**

**重點回顧**

Security professionals often use hashing as a tool to validate the integrity of program files, documents, and other types of data.

安全專業人員經常使用雜湊作為工具來驗證程式檔案、文件和其他類型資料的完整性。

Another way it’s used is to reduce the chances of a data breach.

它的另一種用途是減少資料外洩的機會。

As you’ve learned, not all hashing functions provide the same level of protection.

正如你所學到的，並非所有雜湊函數都提供相同程度的保護。

Rainbow table attacks are more likely to work against algorithms that generate shorter keys, like MD5.

彩虹表攻擊更可能針對產生較短金鑰的演算法起作用，例如 MD5。

Many small- and medium-sized businesses still rely on MD5 to secure sensitive data.

許多中小型企業仍然依賴 MD5 來保護敏感資料。

Knowing about alternative algorithms and salting better prepares you to make impactful security recommendations.

了解替代演算法和加鹽可以讓你更好地提出有效的安全建議。

## Authentication, authorization, and accounting 身份驗證、授權和記帳

### Access controls and authentication systems

### 存取控制和身份驗證系統

Protecting data is a fundamental feature of security controls.

保護資料是安全控制的基本功能。

When it comes to keeping information safe and secure, hashing and encryption are powerful, yet limited tools.

就保持資訊安全而言，雜湊和加密是強大但有限的工具。

Managing who or what has access to information is also key to safeguarding information.

管理誰或什麼有權存取資訊也是保護資訊的關鍵。

The next series of controls that we'll be exploring are access controls, the security controls that manage access, authorization, and accountability of information.

我們接下來要探討的一系列控制措施是存取控制，即管理資訊的存取、授權和問責性的安全控制措施。

When done well, access controls maintain data confidentiality, integrity, and availability.

如果做得好，存取控制可以維護資料的機密性、完整性和可用性。

They also get users the information they need quickly.

它們還可以讓使用者快速獲得所需的資訊。

These systems are commonly broken down into three separate, yet related functions known as the authentication, authorization, and accounting framework.

這些系統通常分為三個獨立但相關的功能，稱為身份驗證、授權和記帳框架。

Each control has its own protocol and systems that make them work.

每個控制都有使其運作的協定和系統。

In this video, let's get comfortable with the basics of the first one on the list, authentication.

在這個影片中，讓我們熟悉清單上的第一個基本概念，即身份驗證。

Authentication systems are access controls that serve a very basic purpose.

身份驗證系統是具有非常基本用途的存取控制。

They ask anything attempting to access information this simple question: who are you?
它們會問任何試圖存取資訊的人這個簡單的問題：你是誰？

Organizations go about collecting answers to these questions differently, depending on the objectives of their security policy.

組織根據其安全策略的目標，以不同的方式收集這些問題的答案。

Some are more thorough than others, but in general, responses to this question can be based on three factors of authentication.

有些比其他更徹底，但總的來說，這個問題的答案可以基於三個身份驗證因素。

The first is knowledge.

第一個是知識。

Authentication by knowledge refers to something the user knows, like a password or the answer to a security question they provided previously.

透過知識進行身份驗證是指使用者知道的東西，例如密碼或他們先前提供的安全問題的答案。

Another factor is ownership, referring to something the user possesses.

另一個因素是擁有，指的是使用者擁有的東西。

A commonly used type of authentication by ownership is a one-time passcode, or OTP.

一種常用的透過擁有進行身份驗證的類型是一次性密碼 (OTP)。

You've probably experienced these at one time or another.

你可能曾經體驗過這些。

They're a random number sequence that an application or website will send you via text or email and ask you to provide.

它們是應用程式或網站透過簡訊或電子郵件發送給你並要求你提供的隨機數字序列。

Last is characteristic.

最後是特徵。

Authentication by this factor is something the user is.

透過此因素進行身份驗證是指使用者本身是什麼。

Biometrics, like fingerprint scans on your smartphone, are example of this type of authentication.

生物識別技術，例如智慧型手機上的指紋掃描，就是此類身份驗證的範例。

While not used everywhere, this form of authentication is becoming more common because it's much tougher for criminals to impersonate someone if they have to mimic a fingerprint or facial scan as opposed to a password.

雖然並非 সর্বত্র 都使用，但這種形式的身份驗證正變得越來越普遍，因為如果罪犯必須模仿指紋或面部掃描而不是密碼，他們就很難冒充他人。

The information provided during authentication needs to match the information on file for these access controls to work.

在身份驗證期間提供的資訊需要與檔案中的資訊匹配，這些存取控制才能運作。

When the credentials don't match, authentication fails and access is denied.

當憑證不匹配時，身份驗證失敗並且存取被拒絕。

When they match, access is granted.

當它們匹配時，存取被授予。

Incorrectly denying access can be frustrating to anyone.

錯誤地拒絕存取可能會讓任何人感到沮喪。

To make access systems more convenient, many organizations these days rely on single sign-on.

為了使存取系統更加方便，現在許多組織都依賴單一登入。

Single sign-on, or SSO, is a technology that combines several different logins into one.

單一登入 (SSO) 是一種將多個不同登入合併為一個的技術。

Can you imagine having to reintroduce yourself every time you meet up with a friend? That's exactly the sort of problem SSO solves.

你能想像每次和朋友見面都必須重新自我介紹嗎？這正是 SSO 解決的問題。

Instead of requiring users to authenticate over and over again, SSO establishes their identity once, allowing them to gain access to company resources faster.

SSO 不是要求使用者一遍又一遍地進行身份驗證，而是建立一次他們的身份，使他們能夠更快地存取公司資源。

While SSO systems are helpful when it comes to speeding up the authentication process, they present a significant vulnerability when used alone.

雖然 SSO 系統在加速身份驗證過程方面很有用，但單獨使用時會產生嚴重的漏洞。

Denying access to authorized users can be frustrating, but you know what's even worse? Incorrectly granting access to the wrong user.

拒絕授權使用者的存取權限可能會令人沮喪，但你知道什麼更糟糕嗎？錯誤地授予錯誤使用者的存取權限。

SSO technology is great, but not if it relies on just a single factor of authentication.

SSO 技術很棒，但如果它僅依賴單一身份驗證因素，那就不好。

Adding more authentication factors strengthen these systems.

添加更多身份驗證因素可以增強這些系統。

Multi-factor authentication, or MFA, is a security measure, which requires a user to verify their identity in two or more ways to access a system or network.

多重身份驗證 (MFA) 是一種安全措施，它要求使用者以兩種或更多種方式驗證其身份才能存取系統或網路。

MFA combines two or more independent credentials, like knowledge and ownership, to prove that someone is who they claim to be.

MFA 結合了兩個或更多個獨立的憑證，例如知識和擁有，以證明某人是他們聲稱的身分。

SSO and MFA are often used in conjunction with one another to layer the defense capabilities of authentication systems.

SSO 和 MFA 通常結合使用，以疊加身份驗證系統的防禦能力。

When both are used, organizations can ensure convenient access that is also secure.

當兩者都使用時，組織可以確保方便且安全的存取。

Now that we covered authentication, we're ready to explore the second part of the framework.

現在我們已經介紹了身份驗證，我們準備好探索框架的第二部分。

Next, we'll learn about authorization!
接下來，我們將學習授權！

### The rise of SSO and MFA SSO 和 MFA 的興起

Most companies help keep their data safely locked up behind authentication systems.

大多數公司都透過身份驗證系統來安全地鎖定其資料。

Usernames and passwords are the keys that unlock information for most organizations.

使用者名稱和密碼是大多數組織解鎖資訊的金鑰。

But are those credentials enough? Information security often focuses on managing a user's access of, and authorization to, information.

但是這些憑證足夠嗎？資訊安全通常側重於管理使用者對資訊的存取和授權。

Previously, you learned about the three factors of authentication: knowledge, ownership, and characteristic.

先前，你學習了身份驗證的三個要素：知識、擁有和特徵。

Single sign-on (SSO) and multi-factor authentication (MFA) are two technologies that have become popular for implementing these authentication factors.

單一登入 (SSO) 和多重身份驗證 (MFA) 是兩種用於實施這些身份驗證要素的流行技術。

In this reading, you’ll learn how these technologies work and why companies are adopting them.

在這篇閱讀中，你將學習這些技術如何運作以及公司採用它們的原因。

**A better approach to authentication**

**更好的身份驗證方法**

Single sign-on (SSO) is a technology that combines several different logins into one.

單一登入 (SSO) 是一種將多個不同登入合併為一個的技術。

More companies are turning to SSO as a solution to their authentication needs for three reasons:
越來越多的公司轉向 SSO 作為其身份驗證需求的解決方案，原因有三：

-   SSO improves the user experience by eliminating the number of usernames and passwords people have to remember.

    SSO 透過減少人們必須記住的使用者名稱和密碼數量來改善使用者體驗。

-   Companies can lower costs by streamlining how they manage connected services.

    公司可以透過簡化管理連接服務的方式來降低成本。

-   SSO improves overall security by reducing the number of access points attackers can target.

    SSO 透過減少攻擊者可以攻擊的存取點數量來提高整體安全性。

This technology became available in the mid-1990s as a way to combat password fatigue, which refers to people’s tendency to reuse passwords across services.

這項技術在 1990 年代中期開始出現，旨在應對密碼疲勞，這是指人們傾向於在不同服務中重複使用密碼。

Remembering many different passwords can be a challenge, but using the same password repeatedly is a major security risk.

記住許多不同的密碼可能是一個挑戰，但重複使用相同的密碼是一個重大的安全風險。

SSO solves this dilemma by shifting the burden of authentication away from the user.

SSO 透過將身份驗證的負擔從使用者身上轉移來解決這個難題。

**How SSO works**

**SSO 如何運作**

SSO works by automating how trust is established between a user and a service provider.

SSO 透過自動化使用者和服務提供者之間如何建立信任來運作。

Rather than placing the responsibility on an employee or customer, SSO solutions use trusted third-parties to prove that a user is who they claim to be.

SSO 解決方案不是將責任放在員工或客戶身上，而是使用受信任的第三方來證明使用者是他們聲稱的身分。

This is done through the exchange of encrypted access tokens between the identity provider and the service provider.

這是透過在身份提供者和服務提供者之間交換加密的存取權杖來完成的。

Similar to other kinds of digital information, these access tokens are exchanged using specific protocols.

與其他類型的數位資訊類似，這些存取權杖是使用特定的協定進行交換的。

SSO implementations commonly rely on two different authentication protocols: LDAP and SAML.

SSO 實作通常依賴兩種不同的身份驗證協定：LDAP 和 SAML。

LDAP, which stands for Lightweight Directory Access Protocol, is mostly used to transmit information on-premises; SAML, which stands for Security Assertion Markup Language, is mostly used to transmit information off-premises, like in the cloud.

LDAP，即輕量級目錄存取協定，主要用於傳輸內部部署的資訊；SAML，即安全聲明標記語言，主要用於傳輸非內部部署的資訊，例如在雲端中。

Note: LDAP and SAML protocols are often used together.

注意：LDAP 和 SAML 協定通常一起使用。

Here's an example of how SSO can connect a user to multiple applications with one access token:

以下是如何使用一個存取權杖將使用者連接到多個應用程式的範例：

One user connects to multiple applications with one access token.

一個使用者使用一個存取權杖連接到多個應用程式。

**Limitations of SSO**

**SSO 的限制**

Usernames and passwords alone are not always the most secure way of protecting sensitive information.

單獨的使用者名稱和密碼並不總是保護敏感資訊的最安全方法。

SSO provides useful benefits, but there’s still the risk associated with using one form of authentication.

SSO 提供了有用的好處，但仍然存在與使用一種形式的身份驗證相關的風險。

For example, a lost or stolen password could expose information across multiple services.

例如，遺失或被盜的密碼可能會洩露多個服務中的資訊。

Thankfully, there’s a solution to this problem.

幸運的是，這個問題有一個解決方案。

**MFA to the rescue**

**MFA 來救援**

Multi-factor authentication (MFA) requires a user to verify their identity in two or more ways to access a system or network.

多重身份驗證 (MFA) 要求使用者以兩種或更多種方式驗證其身份，才能存取系統或網路。

In a sense, MFA is similar to using an ATM to withdraw money from your bank account.

從某種意義上說，MFA 類似於使用 ATM 從你的銀行帳戶提款。

First, you insert a debit card into the machine as one form of identification.

首先，你將金融卡插入機器作為一種身份證明。

Then, you enter your PIN number as a second form of identification.

然後，你輸入你的 PIN 碼作為第二種身份證明。

Combined, both steps, or factors, are used to verify your identity before authorizing you to access the account.

結合起來，這兩個步驟或因素用於在授權你存取帳戶之前驗證你的身份。

An equation showing user login plus biometric or physical devices equal access.

一個顯示使用者登入加上生物識別或實體裝置等於存取的等式。

**Strengthening authentication**

**強化身份驗證**

MFA builds on the benefits of SSO.

MFA 基於 SSO 的優點。

It works by having users prove that they are who they claim to be.

它的運作方式是讓使用者證明他們是他們聲稱的身分。

The user must provide two factors (2FA) or three factors (3FA) to authenticate their identification.

使用者必須提供兩個因素 (2FA) 或三個因素 (3FA) 才能驗證其身份。

The MFA process asks users to provide these proofs, such as:
MFA 流程要求使用者提供這些證明，例如：

-   Something a user knows: most commonly a username and password
    使用者知道的東西：最常見的是使用者名稱和密碼
-   Something a user has: normally received from a service provider, like a one-time passcode (OTP) sent via SMS
    使用者擁有的東西：通常從服務提供者那裡收到，例如透過簡訊發送的一次性密碼 (OTP)
-   Something a user is: refers to physical characteristics of a user, like their fingerprints or facial scans
    使用者本身是什麼：指的是使用者的身體特徵，例如他們的指紋或面部掃描

Requiring multiple forms of identification is an effective security measure, especially in cloud environments.

需要多種形式的身份證明是一種有效的安全措施，尤其是在雲端環境中。

It can be difficult for businesses in the cloud to ensure that the users remotely accessing their systems are not threat actors.

對於雲端中的企業來說，很難確保遠端存取其系統的使用者不是威脅行為者。

MFA can reduce the risk of authenticating the wrong users by requiring forms of identification that are difficult to imitate or brute force.

MFA 可以透過要求難以模仿或暴力破解的身份證明形式來降低驗證錯誤使用者的風險。

**Key takeaways**

**重點回顧**

Implementing both SSO and MFA security controls improves security without sacrificing the user experience.

實施 SSO 和 MFA 安全控制措施既可以提高安全性，又不會犧牲使用者體驗。

Relying on passwords alone is a serious vulnerability.

僅僅依賴密碼是一個嚴重的漏洞。

Implementing SSO means fewer points of entry, but that’s not enough.

實施 SSO 意味著更少的入口點，但這還不夠。

Combining SSO and MFA can be an effective way to protect information, so that users have a streamlined experience while unauthorized people are kept away from important information.

結合 SSO 和 MFA 可能是保護資訊的有效方法，這樣使用者可以擁有簡化的體驗，同時未經授權的人員則無法接觸到重要資訊。

### The mechanisms of authorization 授權機制

Access is as much about authorization as it is about authentication.

存取與授權和身份驗證同樣重要。

One of the most important functions of access controls is how they assign responsibility for certain systems and processes.

存取控制最重要的功能之一是它們如何分配特定系統和流程的責任。

Next up in our exploration of access control systems are the mechanisms of authorization.

在我們對存取控制系統的探索中，接下來是授權機制。

These protocols actually work closely together with authentication technologies.

這些協定實際上與身份驗證技術密切合作。

While one validates who the user is, the other determines what they're allowed to do.

一個驗證使用者是誰，另一個則決定他們被允許做什麼。

Let's take a look at the next part of the authentication, authorization, and accounting framework that protects private information.

讓我們來看看保護私人資訊的身份驗證、授權和記帳框架的下一部分。

Earlier, we learned about the principle of least privilege.

稍早，我們學習了最小權限原則。

Authorization is linked to the idea that access to information only lasts as long as needed.

授權與資訊存取僅在需要時才存在的概念相關聯。

Authorization systems are also heavily influenced by this idea in addition to another important security principle, the separation of duties.

除了另一個重要的安全原則——職責分離之外，授權系統也深受此概念的影響。

Separation of duties is the principle that users should not be given levels of authorization that will allow them to misuse a system.

職責分離是指不應給予使用者允許他們濫用系統的授權級別的原則。

Separating duties reduces the risk of system failures and inappropriate behavior from users.

分離職責可降低系統故障和使用者不當行為的風險。

For example, a person responsible for providing customer service shouldn't also be authorized to rate their own performance.

例如，負責提供客戶服務的人員不應同時被授權評估自己的績效。

In this position, they could easily neglect their duties while continuing to give themselves high marks with no oversight.

在這個職位上，他們可以輕易地忽略自己的職責，同時在沒有監督的情況下繼續給自己高分。

Similarly, if one person was authorized to develop and test a security system, they are much more likely to be unaware of its weaknesses.

同樣地，如果一個人被授權開發和測試安全系統，他們更有可能不知道其弱點。

Both the principle of least privilege and the concept of separating duties apply to more than just people.

最小權限原則和職責分離的概念不僅適用於人。

They apply to all systems including networks, databases, processes, and any other aspect of an organization.

它們適用於所有系統，包括網路、資料庫、流程以及組織的任何其他方面。

Ultimately, authorization depends on a system or user's role.

最終，授權取決於系統或使用者的角色。

When it comes to securing data over a network, there are a couple of frequently used access controls that you should be familiar with: HTTP basic auth and OAuth.

就透過網路保護資料而言，有幾個你應該熟悉的常用存取控制：HTTP 基本驗證和 OAuth。

Have you ever wondered what the HTTP in web addresses stood for.

你是否曾經想過網址中的 HTTP 代表什麼。

It stands for hypertext transfer protocol, which is how communications are established over network.

它代表超文本傳輸協定，這是如何在網路上建立通訊的方式。

HTTP uses what is known as basic auth, the technology used to establish a user's request to access a server.

HTTP 使用稱為基本驗證的技術，該技術用於建立使用者存取伺服器的請求。

Basic auth works by sending an identifier every time a user communicates with a web page.

基本驗證的工作方式是在使用者每次與網頁通訊時傳送一個識別碼。

Some websites still use basic auth to tell whether or not someone is authorized to access information on that site.

一些網站仍然使用基本驗證來判斷某人是否被授權存取該網站上的資訊。

However, their protocol is considered to be vulnerable to attacks because it transmits usernames and password openly over the network.

然而，它們的協定被認為容易受到攻擊，因為它會在網路上公開傳輸使用者名稱和密碼。

Most websites today use HTTPS instead, which stands for hypertext transfer protocol secure.

如今，大多數網站都改用 HTTPS，它代表安全的超文本傳輸協定。

This protocol doesn't expose sensitive information, like access credentials, when communicating over the network.

此協定在透過網路通訊時不會洩露敏感資訊，例如存取憑證。

Another secure authentication technology used today is OAuth.

如今使用的另一種安全身份驗證技術是 OAuth。

OAuth is an open-standard authorization protocol that shares designated access between applications.

OAuth 是一種開放標準的授權協定，可在應用程式之間共享指定的存取權限。

For example, you can tell Google that it's okay for another website to access your profile to create an account.

例如，你可以告訴 Google，允許另一個網站存取你的個人資料以建立帳戶。

Instead of requesting and sending sensitive usernames and passwords over the network, OAuth uses API tokens to verify access between you and a service provider.

OAuth 不是在網路上請求和傳送敏感的使用者名稱和密碼，而是使用 API 權杖來驗證你和服務提供者之間的存取權限。

An API token is a small block of encrypted code that contains information about a user.

API 權杖是一小段包含使用者資訊的加密程式碼。

These tokens contain things like your identity, site permissions, and more.

這些權杖包含你的身分、網站權限等等。

OAuth sends and receives access requests using API tokens by passing them from a server to a user's device.

OAuth 透過將 API 權杖從伺服器傳遞到使用者的裝置來傳送和接收存取請求。

Let's explore what's going on behind the scenes.

讓我們來探討幕後發生了什麼。

When you authorize a site to create an account using your Google profile, all of Google's usual login protocols are still active.

當你授權某個網站使用你的 Google 個人資料建立帳戶時，Google 所有常用的登入協定仍然有效。

If you have multi-factor authentication enabled on your account, and you should, you'll still have the security benefits that it provides.

如果你的帳戶啟用了多重身份驗證（你應該這樣做），你仍然可以擁有它提供的安全優勢。

API tokens minimize risks in a major way.

API 權杖在很大程度上最大限度地降低了風險。

These API tokens serve as an additional layer of encryption that helps to keep your Google password safe in the event of a breach on another platform.

這些 API 權杖作為額外的加密層，有助於在另一個平台上發生洩露時保護你的 Google 密碼安全。

Basic auth and OAuth are just a couple of examples of authorization tools that are designed with the principles of least privilege and separation of duty in mind.

基本驗證和 OAuth 只是以最小權限和職責分離原則設計的授權工具的幾個範例。

There are many other controls that help limit the risk of unauthorized access to information.

還有許多其他控制措施有助於限制未經授權存取資訊的風險。

In addition to controlling access, it's also important to monitor it.

除了控制存取之外，監控存取也很重要。

### Why we audit user activity 我們為什麼要稽核使用者活動

Have you ever wondered if your employer is keeping a record of when you log into company systems? Well, they are, if they're implementing the third and final function of the authentication, authorization, and accounting framework.

你是否曾想過你的雇主是否會記錄你何時登入公司系統？嗯，如果他們正在實施身份驗證、授權和記帳框架的第三個也是最後一個功能，他們就會這樣做。

Accounting is the practice of monitoring the access logs of a system.

記帳是監控系統存取日誌的實務。

These logs contain information like who accessed the system, and when they accessed it, and what resources they used.

這些日誌包含諸如誰存取了系統、他們何時存取了系統以及他們使用了哪些資源等資訊。

Security analysts use access logs a lot.

安全分析師經常使用存取日誌。

The data they contain is a helpful way to identify trends, like failed login attempts.

它們包含的資料是識別趨勢（例如失敗的登入嘗試）的有用方法。

They're also used to uncover hackers who have gained access to a system, and for detecting an incident, like a data breach.

它們也用於揭露已獲得系統存取權限的駭客，以及偵測事件，例如資料外洩。

In this field, access logs are essential.

在這個領域，存取日誌至關重要。

Oftentimes, analyzing them is the first procedure you'll follow when investigating a security event.

通常，分析它們是你調查安全事件時要遵循的第一個程序。

So, how do access logs compile all this useful information? Let's examine this more closely.

那麼，存取日誌如何編譯所有這些有用的資訊？讓我們仔細研究一下。

Anytime a user accesses a system, they initiate what's called a session.

每當使用者存取系統時，他們就會啟動一個稱為會期的動作。

A session is a sequence of network HTTP basic auth requests and responses associated with the same user, like when you visit a website.

會期是與同一使用者相關聯的一系列網路 HTTP 基本驗證請求和回應，就像你造訪網站時一樣。

Access logs are essentially records of sessions that capture the moment a user enters a system until the moment they leave it.

存取日誌本質上是會期的記錄，記錄了使用者進入系統的那一刻直到他們離開的那一刻。

Two actions are triggered when the session begins.

當會期開始時，會觸發兩個動作。

The first is the creation of a session ID.

第一個是建立會期 ID。

A session ID is a unique token that identifies a user and their device while accessing the system.

會期 ID 是一個唯一的權杖，用於在使用者存取系統時識別使用者及其裝置。

Session IDs are attached to the user until they either close their browser or the session times out.

會期 ID 會附加到使用者身上，直到他們關閉瀏覽器或會期逾時。

The second action that takes place at the start of a session is an exchange of session cookies between a server and a user's device.

在會期開始時發生的第二個動作是伺服器和使用者裝置之間交換會期 Cookie。

A session cookie is a token that websites use to validate a session and determine how long that session should last.

會期 Cookie 是網站用來驗證會期並確定該會期應持續多長時間的權杖。

When cookies are exchanged between your computer and a server, your session ID is read to determine what information the website should show you.

當 Cookie 在你的電腦和伺服器之間交換時，會讀取你的會期 ID 以確定網站應向你顯示哪些資訊。

Cookies make web sessions safer and more efficient.

Cookie 使網路會期更安全、更有效率。

The exchange of tokens means that no sensitive information, like usernames and passwords, are shared.

權杖的交換意味著不會共享敏感資訊，例如使用者名稱和密碼。

Session cookies prevent attackers from obtaining sensitive data.

會期 Cookie 可防止攻擊者取得敏感資料。

However, there's other damage that they can do.

但是，它們可能會造成其他損害。

With a stolen cookie, an attacker can impersonate a user using their session token.

有了被盜的 Cookie，攻擊者可以使用其會期權杖來冒充使用者。

This kind of attack is known as session hijacking.

這種攻擊稱為會期劫持。

Session hijacking is an event when attackers obtain a legitimate user's session ID.

會期劫持是指攻擊者取得合法使用者的會期 ID 的事件。

During these kinds of attacks, cyber criminals impersonate the user, causing all sorts of harm.

在這些類型的攻擊中，網路罪犯會冒充使用者，造成各種損害。

Money or private data can be stolen.

金錢或私人資料可能會被竊取。

If, for example, hijackers obtain a single sign-on credential from stolen cookies, they can even gain access to additional systems that otherwise seem secure.

例如，如果劫持者從被盜的 Cookie 中獲得單一登入憑證，他們甚至可以存取其他看似安全的系統。

This is one reason why accounting and monitoring session logs is so important.

這就是為什麼記帳和監控會期日誌如此重要的原因之一。

Unusual activity on access logs can be an indication that information has been improperly accessed or stolen.

存取日誌上的異常活動可能表示資訊已被不當存取或竊取。

At the end of the day, accounting is how we gain valuable insight that makes information safer.

歸根究柢，記帳是我們獲得寶貴見解以使資訊更安全的方式。

### Identity and access management 身分與存取管理

Security is more than simply combining processes and technologies to protect assets.

安全不僅僅是結合流程和技術來保護資產。

Instead, security is about ensuring that these processes and technologies are creating a secure environment that supports a defense strategy.

相反地，安全是關於確保這些流程和技術正在創建一個支援防禦策略的安全環境。

A key to doing this is implementing two fundamental security principles that limit access to organizational resources:
做到這一點的關鍵是實施兩個限制存取組織資源的基本安全原則：

-   The principle of least privilege in which a user is only granted the minimum level of access and authorization required to complete a task or function.

    最小權限原則，即僅授予使用者完成任務或功能所需的最低存取和授權級別。

-   Separation of duties, which is the principle that users should not be given levels of authorization that would allow them to misuse a system.

    職責分離，即不應給予使用者允許他們濫用系統的授權級別的原則。

Both principles typically support each other.

這兩個原則通常會相互支持。

For example, according to least privilege, a person who needs permission to approve purchases from the IT department shouldn't have the permission to approve purchases from every department.

例如，根據最小權限原則，需要獲得批准 IT 部門採購許可的人不應擁有批准每個部門採購的許可。

Likewise, according to separation of duties, the person who can approve purchases from the IT department should be different from the person who can input new purchases.

同樣地，根據職責分離，可以批准 IT 部門採購的人應該與可以輸入新採購的人不同。

In other words, least privilege limits the access that an individual receives, while separation of duties divides responsibilities among multiple people to prevent any one person from having too much control.

換句話說，最小權限限制了個人獲得的存取權限，而職責分離則在多人之間分配責任，以防止任何一個人擁有過多的控制權。

Note: Separation of duties is sometimes referred to as segregation of duties.

注意：職責分離有時也稱為職務劃分。

Previously, you learned about the authentication, authorization, and accounting (AAA) framework.

先前，你學習了身份驗證、授權和記帳 (AAA) 框架。

Many businesses used this model to implement these two security principles and manage user access.

許多企業使用此模型來實施這兩個安全原則並管理使用者存取。

In this reading, you’ll learn about the other major framework for managing user access, identity and access management (IAM).

在這篇閱讀中，你將學習另一個用於管理使用者存取的主要框架，即身分與存取管理 (IAM)。

You will learn about the similarities between AAA and IAM and how they're commonly implemented.

你將學習 AAA 和 IAM 之間的相似之處以及它們的常見實作方式。

**Identity and access management (IAM)**

**身分與存取管理 (IAM)**

As organizations become more reliant on technology, regulatory agencies have put more pressure on them to demonstrate that they’re doing everything they can to prevent threats.

隨著組織越來越依賴技術，監管機構對它們施加了更大的壓力，要求它們證明自己正在盡一切努力來預防威脅。

Identity and access management (IAM) is a collection of processes and technologies that helps organizations manage digital identities in their environment.

身分與存取管理 (IAM) 是一系列流程和技術，可幫助組織管理其環境中的數位身分。

Both AAA and IAM systems are designed to authenticate users, determine their access privileges, and track their activities within a system.

AAA 和 IAM 系統都旨在驗證使用者身分、確定其存取權限並追蹤其在系統中的活動。

Either model used by your organization is more than a single, clearly defined system.

你的組織使用的任何一種模型都不僅僅是一個單一、明確定義的系統。

They each consist of a collection of security controls that ensure the right user is granted access to the right resources at the right time and for the right reasons.

它們各自包含一系列安全控制，以確保在正確的時間以正確的理由授予正確的使用者存取正確的資源的權限。

Each of those four factors is determined by your organization's policies and processes.

這四個因素中的每一個都由你組織的政策和流程決定。

Note: A user can either be a person, a device, or software.

注意：使用者可以是人、裝置或軟體。

**Authenticating users**

**驗證使用者身分**

To ensure the right user is attempting to access a resource requires some form of proof that the user is who they claim to be.

為了確保正確的使用者正試圖存取資源，需要某種形式的證明來證明使用者是他們聲稱的身分。

In a
video on authentication controls
, you learned that there are a few factors that can be used to authenticate a user:
在關於身份驗證控制的影片中，你學到可以使用一些因素來驗證使用者身分：

-   Knowledge, or something the user knows
    知識，或使用者知道的東西
-   Ownership, or something the user possesses
    擁有，或使用者擁有的東西
-   Characteristic, or something the user is
    特徵，或使用者本身是什麼

Authentication is mainly verified with login credentials.

身份驗證主要透過登入憑證進行驗證。

Single sign-on (SSO), a technology that combines several different logins into one, and multi-factor authentication (MFA), a security measure that requires a user to verify their identity in two or more ways to access a system or network, are other tools that organizations use to authenticate individuals and systems.

單一登入 (SSO)，一種將多個不同登入合併為一個的技術，以及多重身份驗證 (MFA)，一種要求使用者以兩種或更多種方式驗證其身分才能存取系統或網路的安全措施，是組織用來驗證個人和系統身分的其他工具。

Pro tip: Another way to remember this authentication model is: something you know, something you have, and something you are.

專家提示：記住此身份驗證模型的另一種方法是：你所知道的東西、你擁有的東西和你本身是什麼。

**User provisioning**

**使用者佈建**

Back-end systems need to be able to verify whether the information provided by a user is accurate.

後端系統需要能夠驗證使用者提供的資訊是否準確。

To accomplish this, users must be properly provisioned.

為了實現這一點，必須正確佈建使用者。

User provisioning is the process of creating and maintaining a user's digital identity.

使用者佈建是建立和維護使用者數位身分的過程。

For example, a college might create a new user account when a new instructor is hired.

例如，學院可能會在新講師受聘時建立一個新的使用者帳戶。

The new account will be configured to provide access to instructor-only resources while they are teaching.

新帳戶將配置為在他們教學期間提供對僅限講師使用的資源的存取權限。

Security analysts are routinely involved with provisioning users and their access privileges.

安全分析師經常參與佈建使用者及其存取權限。

Pro tip: Another role analysts have in IAM is to deprovision users.

專家提示：分析師在 IAM 中扮演的另一個角色是取消佈建使用者。

This is an important practice that removes a user's access rights when they should no longer have them.

這是一個重要的實務，可以在使用者不再應該擁有存取權限時移除其存取權。

**Granting authorization**

**授予授權**

If the right user has been authenticated, the network should ensure the right resources are made available.

如果已驗證了正確的使用者身分，則網路應確保提供正確的資源。

There are three common frameworks that organizations use to handle this step of IAM:
組織使用三種常見的框架來處理 IAM 的此步驟：

-   Mandatory access control (MAC)
    強制存取控制 (MAC)
-   Discretionary access control (DAC)
    自主存取控制 (DAC)
-   Role-based access control (RBAC)
    角色型存取控制 (RBAC)

A system administrator deciding to grant users and an operating system access to data.

系統管理員決定授予使用者和作業系統資料存取權限。

**Mandatory Access Control (MAC)**

**強制存取控制 (MAC)**

MAC is the strictest of the three frameworks.

MAC 是這三個框架中最嚴格的。

Authorization in this model is based on a strict need-to-know basis.

此模型中的授權基於嚴格的「需要知道」原則。

Access to information must be granted manually by a central authority or system administrator.

資訊存取必須由中央授權機構或系統管理員手動授予。

For example, MAC is commonly applied in law enforcement, military, and other government agencies where users must request access through a chain of command.

例如，MAC 通常應用於執法部門、軍事部門和其他政府機構，在這些機構中，使用者必須透過指揮系統請求存取權限。

MAC is also known as non-discretionary control because access isn’t given at the discretion of the data owner.

MAC 也稱為非自主控制，因為存取權不是由資料擁有者自行決定授予的。

A data owner choosing to grant specific users access to their data.

資料擁有者選擇授予特定使用者對其資料的存取權限。

**Discretionary Access Control (DAC)**

**自主存取控制 (DAC)**

DAC is typically applied when a data owner decides appropriate levels of access.

當資料擁有者決定適當的存取級別時，通常會應用 DAC。

One example of DAC is when the owner of a Google Drive folder shares editor, viewer, or commentor access with someone else.

DAC 的一個範例是當 Google 雲端硬碟資料夾的擁有者與其他人共享編輯者、檢視者或評論者存取權限時。

A system administrator assigning users to specific roles that have predefined access levels.

系統管理員將使用者分配到具有預定義存取級別的特定角色。

**Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)**

**角色型存取控制 (RBAC)**

RBAC is used when authorization is determined by a user's role within an organization.

當授權由使用者在組織內的角色決定時，會使用 RBAC。

For example, a user in the marketing department may have access to user analytics but not network administration.

例如，行銷部門的使用者可能有權存取使用者分析，但無權存取網路管理。

**Access control technologies**

**存取控制技術**

Users often experience authentication and authorization as a single, seamless experience.

使用者通常會將身份驗證和授權視為單一、無縫的體驗。

In large part, that’s due to access control technologies that are configured to work together.

很大程度上，這是由於配置為協同工作的存取控制技術。

These tools offer the speed and automation needed by administrators to monitor and modify access rights.

這些工具提供了管理員監控和修改存取權限所需的速度和自動化功能。

They also decrease errors and potential risks.

它們還減少了錯誤和潛在風險。

An organization's IT department sometimes develops and maintains customized access control technologies on their own.

組織的 IT 部門有時會自行開發和維護客製化的存取控制技術。

A typical IAM or AAA system consists of a user directory, a set of tools for managing data in that directory, an authorization system, and an auditing system.

典型的 IAM 或 AAA 系統包含使用者目錄、一組用於管理該目錄中資料的工具、一個授權系統和一個稽核系統。

Some organizations create custom systems to tailor them to their security needs.

一些組織會建立客製化系統以使其符合其安全需求。

However, building an in-house solution comes at a steep cost of time and other resources.

然而，建立內部解決方案需要付出高昂的時間和其他資源成本。

Instead, many organizations opt to license third-party solutions that offer a suite of tools that enable them to quickly secure their information systems.

相反地，許多組織選擇授權第三方解決方案，這些解決方案提供一套工具，使他們能夠快速保護其資訊系統。

Keep in mind, security is about more than combining a bunch of tools.

請記住，安全不僅僅是結合一堆工具。

It’s always important to configure these technologies so they can help to provide a secure environment.

始終重要的是要配置這些技術，以便它們可以幫助提供安全的環境。

### Glossary terms from module 2 模組 2 的詞彙表

**Terms and definitions from Course 5, Module 2**

**課程 5，模組 2 的術語和定義**

**Access controls:** Security controls that manage access, authorization, and accountability of information

**存取控制：**管理資訊的存取、授權和問責性的安全控制

**Algorithm:** A set of rules used to solve a problem

**演算法：**用於解決問題的一組規則

**Application programming interface (API) token:** A small block of encrypted code that contains information about a user

**應用程式介面 (API) 權杖：** 包含使用者資訊的一小段加密程式碼

**Asymmetric encryption:** The use of a public and private key pair for encryption and decryption of data

**非對稱加密：** 使用一對公開金鑰和私密金鑰來加密和解密資料

**Basic auth:** The technology used to establish a user’s request to access a server

**基本驗證：** 用於建立使用者存取伺服器請求的技術

**Bit:** The smallest unit of data measurement on a computer

**位元：** 電腦上最小的資料測量單位

**Brute force attack:** The trial and error process of discovering private information

**暴力破解攻擊：** 發現私人資訊的反覆試驗過程

**Cipher:** An algorithm that encrypts information

**密碼：** 一種加密資訊的演算法

**Cryptographic key:** A mechanism that decrypts ciphertext

**密碼金鑰：** 一種解密密文的機制

**Cryptography:** The process of transforming information into a form that unintended readers can’t understand

**密碼學：** 將資訊轉換為非預期讀者無法理解的形式的過程

**Data custodian:** Anyone or anything that’s responsible for the safe handling, transport, and storage of information

**資料管理員：** 任何負責安全處理、傳輸和儲存資訊的人或物

**Data owner:** The person that decides who can access, edit, use, or destroy their information

**資料擁有者：** 決定誰可以存取、編輯、使用或銷毀其資訊的人

**Digital certificate:** A file that verifies the identity of a public key holder

**數位憑證：** 驗證公開金鑰持有者身分的檔案

**Encryption:** The process of converting data from a readable format to an encoded format

**加密：** 將資料從可讀格式轉換為編碼格式的過程

**Hash collision:** An instance when different inputs produce the same hash value

**雜湊碰撞：** 不同輸入產生相同雜湊值的情況

**Hash function:** An algorithm that produces a code that can’t be decrypted

**雜湊函數：** 產生無法解密的程式碼的演算法

**Hash table:** A data structure that's used to store and reference hash values

**雜湊表：** 用於儲存和參考雜湊值的資料結構

**Identity and access management (IAM):** A collection of processes and technologies that helps organizations manage digital identities in their environment

**身分與存取管理 (IAM)：** 一系列流程和技術，可幫助組織管理其環境中的數位身分

**Information privacy:** The protection of unauthorized access and distribution of data

**資訊隱私：** 保護未經授權的資料存取和散佈

**Multi-factor authentication (MFA):** A security measure that requires a user to verify their identity in two or more ways to access a system or network

**多重身份驗證 (MFA)：** 一種安全措施，要求使用者以兩種或更多種方式驗證其身份才能存取系統或網路

**Non-repudiation:** The concept that the authenticity of information can’t be denied

**不可否認性：** 資訊的真實性不能被否認的概念

**OAuth:** An open-standard authorization protocol that shares designated access between applications

**OAuth：** 一種開放標準的授權協定，可在應用程式之間共享指定的存取權限

**Payment Card Industry Data Security Standards (PCI DSS):** A set of security standards formed by major organizations in the financial industry

**支付卡產業資料安全標準 (PCI DSS)：** 由金融業主要組織制定的一組安全標準

**Personally identifiable information (PII):** Any information used to infer an individual's identity

**個人身份資訊 (PII)：** 用於推斷個人身分的任何資訊

**Principle of least privilege:** The concept of granting only the minimal access and authorization required to complete a task or function

**最小權限原則：** 僅授予完成任務或功能所需的最低存取權限和授權的概念

**Protected health information (PHI):** Information that relates to the past, present, or future physical or mental health or condition of an individual

**受保護的健康資訊 (PHI)：** 與個人過去、現在或未來的身心健康或狀況相關的資訊

**Public key infrastructure (PKI):** An encryption framework that secures the exchange of online information

**公開金鑰基礎建設 (PKI)：** 一種保護線上資訊交換的加密框架

**Rainbow table:** A file of pre-generated hash values and their associated plaintext

**彩虹表：** 包含預先產生的雜湊值及其相關明文的檔案

**Salting:** An additional safeguard that’s used to strengthen hash functions

**加鹽：** 用於增強雜湊函數的額外安全措施

**Security assessment:** A check to determine how resilient current security implementations are against threats

**安全評估：** 檢查以確定目前的安全實作對威脅的抵抗力如何

**Security audit:** A review of an organization's security controls, policies, and procedures against a set of expectations

**安全稽核：** 根據一組預期來審查組織的安全控制、政策和程序

**Security controls:** Safeguards designed to reduce specific security risks

**安全控制：** 旨在降低特定安全風險的保護措施

**Separation of duties:** The principle that users should not be given levels of authorization that would allow them to misuse a system

**職責分離：** 不應給予使用者允許他們濫用系統的授權級別的原則

**Session:** A sequence of network HTTP basic auth requests and responses associated with the same user

**會期：** 與同一使用者相關聯的一系列網路 HTTP 基本驗證請求和回應

**Session cookie:** A token that websites use to validate a session and determine how long that session should last

**會期 Cookie：** 網站用來驗證會期並決定該會期應持續多長時間的權杖

**Session hijacking:** An event when attackers obtain a legitimate user’s session ID

**會期劫持：** 攻擊者取得合法使用者會期 ID 的事件

**Session ID:** A unique token that identifies a user and their device while accessing a system

**會期 ID：** 在使用者存取系統時識別使用者及其裝置的唯一權杖

**Single Sign-On (SSO):** A technology that combines several different logins into one

**單一登入 (SSO)：** 將多個不同登入合併為一個的技術

**Symmetric encryption:** The use of a single secret key to exchange information

**對稱加密：** 使用單一密鑰來交換資訊

**User provisioning:** The process of creating and maintaining a user's digital identity

**使用者佈建：** 建立和維護使用者數位身分的過程
